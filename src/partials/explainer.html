Reading security evidence against the ASD Essentials

Three invented pieces of evidence, each placed against the ASD Essentials for enterprise IT. The framework has three layers.

  1. Principles describe the cyber security outcomes an organisation should achieve.
  2. Actions describe what needs to be done to achieve those outcomes.
  3. Domains describe the business functions and assets to which the actions apply.

For each action, "Assessors consider coverage, consistency, currency and evidence quality when determining an action rating." The four rows under each sample follow those four considerations.

Sample 1 of 3Conditional Access policy export

Where it could map in the ASD Essentials for enterprise IT
Principle
PRO-13Identity, credential and access management
Action
Use phishing-resistant MFA for users authenticating to systems, including from untrusted, external or high-risk locations.
Domain
Identity servicesASD describes this domain as: "Enterprise-wide identity, credential and access management services such as directories, authentication and authorisation services or credential stores."

One possible mapping, chosen for this illustration.

The four considerations
  • Coverage

    Shows The policy targets all users and all cloud apps, with one excluded group of 2 accounts.

    Cannot show Whether another sign-in path, such as legacy authentication, sits outside this policy.

  • Consistency

    Shows One policy with one grant control, stated plainly.

    Cannot show Whether guests, administrators and other tenants are treated the same way.

  • Currency

    Shows The policy was switched on when the export was taken on 2026-10-20.

    Cannot show Whether anything has changed since that date.

  • Evidence quality

    Shows An export of settings is a direct record of configuration.

    Cannot show What people actually used when they signed in.

A plain reading

Can be read from the document

Where an assessment could be reasonably confident

  • MFA is required for all users except one named group.
  • SMS is an allowed method alongside stronger ones, so the policy as written does not require phishing-resistant MFA only.

Cannot be known from this document

Where an assessment would be uncertain

  • Which method each person actually uses.
  • Whether the exclusion is justified and who approved it.
What a person would check next
  1. Read the sign-in logs for the last 30 days to see which methods people used.
  2. Check who is in the excluded group and who approved the exclusion.
  3. Look for other policies or legacy sign-in routes that go around this one.

Why a person reviews it. ASD says that where existing evidence demonstrates "deployment but not sustained effectiveness", organisations may need additional operational evidence.

Sample 2 of 3Monitoring agent inventory

Where it could map in the ASD Essentials for enterprise IT
Principle
DET-05Detection capability efficacy
Action
Review detection coverage periodically to identify gaps arising from new assets, services or architectural changes.
Domain
Visibility and responseASD describes this domain as: "Procedures and capabilities for detecting, analysing and responding to cyber security events and incidents."

One possible mapping, chosen for this illustration.

The four considerations
  • Coverage

    Shows 9 of the 12 listed endpoints reported on the report date.

    Cannot show Whether the 12 listed are every endpoint the organisation has. A device missing from the list would not appear.

  • Consistency

    Shows The 9 reporting endpoints run the same agent version.

    Cannot show Whether they send the same kinds of logs or use the same settings.

  • Currency

    Shows Two endpoints last checked in on 2026-08-14 and 2026-09-02.

    Cannot show Whether the 9 reporting endpoints have reported every day, or only on the report date.

  • Evidence quality

    Shows Counts and dates, recorded by the tool that manages the agents.

    Cannot show Whether a check-in means events are collected, detected on and reviewed by anyone.

A plain reading

Can be read from the document

Where an assessment could be reasonably confident

  • 9 of 12 listed endpoints reported on the report date.
  • Two endpoints have not checked in for some weeks, and one has no agent.

Cannot be known from this document

Where an assessment would be uncertain

  • Whether the list is complete.
  • Whether the reporting agents give useful detection.
What a person would check next
  1. Compare the inventory with the asset register and the directory to find devices that are not listed.
  2. Find out why the 3 endpoints are not reporting and whether they are still in use.
  3. Send a harmless test event from one endpoint and confirm it reaches the console and is seen.

Why a person reviews it. ASD says that where existing evidence demonstrates "deployment but not sustained effectiveness", organisations may need additional operational evidence.

Sample 3 of 3Incident response plan cover page

Where it could map in the ASD Essentials for enterprise IT
Principle
RES-03Cyber security incident response
Action
Use documented, tested and approved cyber security incident response procedures when responding to cyber security incidents.
Domain
Visibility and responseASD describes this domain as: "Procedures and capabilities for detecting, analysing and responding to cyber security events and incidents."

One possible mapping, chosen for this illustration.

The four considerations
  • Coverage

    Shows A plan exists, with a version number and a named approver.

    Cannot show Which systems, teams and kinds of incident the plan covers. This is only the cover page.

  • Consistency

    Shows Nothing on the page contradicts itself.

    Cannot show Whether the steps in the plan match what the team really does.

  • Currency

    Shows The approval date is 2024-06-12, more than two years old, and no next review is recorded.

    Cannot show Whether the plan has been used or updated outside this document.

  • Evidence quality

    Shows A formal approval, recorded on a controlled document.

    Cannot show Whether the plan was ever tested. "No record attached" means this page has none, not that no exercise took place.

A plain reading

Can be read from the document

Where an assessment could be reasonably confident

  • The plan is documented and approved.
  • The approval is more than two years old and no test is recorded on this page.

Cannot be known from this document

Where an assessment would be uncertain

  • Whether the plan has been tested somewhere else.
  • Whether people know the plan and could follow it.
What a person would check next
  1. Ask for exercise or test reports, or notes from any real incident the plan was used in.
  2. Look at the revision history for changes made after 2024-06-12.
  3. Check that the roles and contact details in the plan are still correct.

Why a person reviews it. ASD says that where existing evidence demonstrates "deployment but not sustained effectiveness", organisations may need additional operational evidence.
